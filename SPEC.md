# Tradimation — Authored Drawing Effects v5

This revision covers **Smear**, **Suck In**, **Spit Out**, **Launch Away**, **Stretch Wipe**, and **Stamp In**. All six effects share one rendering rule: the live HTML element is captured once, uploaded as a single canvas texture, deformed continuously, and returned to live HTML without a visible swap.

## Framework-neutral library

The catalog runs through `@tradimation/core`, a dependency-free TypeScript package rather than catalog-local animation functions. Its public API accepts existing `HTMLElement` instances, resolves one of 16 curated manifest-backed definitions, and returns an imperative controller with play, pause, reverse, seek, finish, cancel, playback-rate, and destroy operations. DOM/SVG effects use WAAPI; the six deformation effects use the connected WebGL renderer. Framework integrations remain optional lifecycle adapters over this API.

## Complete effect catalog

`catalog.html` is a compact index rather than a wall of embedded demos. Each retained definition has a dedicated page under `effects/` with its actual-library preview, rationale, snippet, and adjacent navigation. Effects cover reusable visual behavior; recipes document contextual control, state, entrance, exit, and spatial usage.

## Programmable motion lab

Every effect is driven by a typed constant set rather than an inaccessible fixed animation clip. The HTML catalog generates a slider panel from `CONTROL_SCHEMA` for all six effects and passes a snapshot of the current values into its motion mapper on replay.

- Duration, force, deformation, cadence, phase timing, and restoration constants expose the dominant behavior of each effect.
- Slider input persists under the local `tradimation-controls` browser key, with one-time fallback to the previous `cel-motion-controls` key, and queues a fresh replay after adjustment.
- Per-effect reset restores one constant set; Reset all restores the authored baseline for the complete catalog.
- Copy values exports the current six constant sets as JSON so a tuned result can be promoted back into authored defaults.
- Timing sliders are constrained at evaluation time so dependent phases retain a valid order even when users choose overlapping raw values.
- A replay snapshots its constants at the start, preventing a dragged slider from changing the middle of the currently visible drawing.

## Continuity audit

The initial prototype felt stepped for four concrete reasons:

- Each labeled phase restarted its own `ease` curve, unintentionally reducing velocity to zero at most boundaries.
- Translation and deformation changed phases on the same exact tick instead of overlapping through the handoff.
- Per-column effects used broad synchronized thresholds, so many mesh vertices changed acceleration together.
- Multiple visible studies could compete for animation frames during catalog playback.

The revised runtime uses C1-continuous Hermite trajectories for primary travel, fifth-order smooth envelopes for deformation, staggered per-column response windows, and viewport-scoped autoplay. Intentional discontinuity remains only where contact or a planted leading edge supplies a physical reason for it.

## Traditional-animation audit

The first cadence pass removed 60 fps interpolation from deformation, but it did not change the underlying performance. The result was the same procedural elastic motion displayed on fewer frames. Three structural problems remained:

- Most silhouettes were generated by one smooth pulse, so anticipation, extreme, recoil, and settle were merely different amounts of the same shape.
- Sine curves and broad per-column easing made every part of the surface behave like soft material, even when the action required a rigid take, wedge, spear, contact, or planted edge.
- Overshoot was treated as a decaying value rather than a contrasting drawing. This produced small continuous wobble instead of a readable squash/rebound statement.

The revised effects use **pose families**. Each action has a deliberately different graphic silhouette, and adjacent drawings change role rather than merely amplitude:

1. **Read / contact drawing** — simple and rigid enough to establish the object.
2. **Take drawing** — favors direction and clean spacing over gradual deformation.
3. **Extreme drawing** — one unmistakable long, wedge, spear, squash, or stretch silhouette.
4. **Opposing drawing** — brake, recoil, catch, or undershoot changes the line of action.
5. **Registration drawing** — removes residual energy and matches the live DOM exactly.

Primary travel and major deformation remain continuous because browser frame skipping is not traditional animation. Exposure timing is reserved for small graphic accents; contrasting silhouettes, asymmetry, unequal spacing, and overlapping forces create the performance.

## Traditional shape-exposure pass

The expert review found that fully smooth shape interpolation still read as digital morphing even after the pose families were improved. The runtime now keeps center-of-mass travel on continuous Hermite trajectories while blending each deformation envelope with an 18 fps exposure clock. Only 55% of shape time is exposed, and the remainder stays continuous, so strong drawings become legible without making the trajectory stutter.

- Smear no longer compresses below the original width or reverses position during braking.
- Suck In and Spit Out use rear/front silhouette boundaries instead of liquid-like per-column convergence.
- Launch Away uses a monotonic exit trajectory and completes its speed drawing inside the visible travel window.
- Stretch Wipe restores width and height monotonically while its center is still decelerating.
- Stamp In removes the secondary width and height overshoot after the planted squash.

### Independent follow-up pass

A second visual review tightened the distinction between continuous travel and exposed drawings. Shape exposure now carries more weight without quantizing center-of-mass motion. Launch spacing is normalized against the available stage distance, Spit Out releases columns front-to-back, and Suck In clamps its leading stretch before a delayed column ingestion. Stretch Wipe separates its front overshoot from rear catch, while Stamp In derives the hanging pose from the element's true bottom edge so the complete anticipation drawing remains visible.

## Traditional performance pass — v5

The visual audit compared eight normalized frames from every effect. It confirmed that cadence alone was not enough: Smear, Launch Away, and Stamp In still read mainly as uniform rectangle scaling, while Suck In, Spit Out, and Stretch Wipe changed shape through broad gradients with weak graphic accents. The v5 pass therefore changes the deformation vocabulary rather than reducing the frame rate again.

| Effect | Previous read | v5 drawing performance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Smear | Long rectangle followed by a smooth width pulse | A low speed drawing has tapered ends and a straight line of action; a narrow rear-to-front compression drawing interrupts it; a deliberately tall recoil removes the remaining energy. |
| Suck In | One continuously narrowing funnel | The body first resists as a rigid block, a thin tongue makes contact, broad wedge drawings hand off the mass, and the far edge finishes as a spear. |
| Spit Out | Funnel played outward with a landing scale | A pressure dot changes into a spear, then a one-sided wedge, then a readable airborne block before contact, squash, and rebound. |
| Launch Away | Uniform horizontal stretch | A compact take changes into one held aerodynamic drawing with a dragged tail, solid nose, straight directional tilt, and increasingly open spacing. |
| Stretch Wipe | Smoothly expanding quadrilateral | Alternating slanted leading-edge drawings write the surface; a restrained corner accent distinguishes the drawings while both boundaries remain connected. |
| Stamp In | Centered scale bounce | A narrow contact drawing precedes a bottom-planted squash with a middle-weighted belly, followed by a tall recoil and one smaller opposing registration drawing. |

The controlling rule is **pose contrast before interpolation quality**. Every effect must contain at least one drawing whose line of action opposes the previous drawing. This contrast may be long/low → compact/tall, block → wedge → spear, or contact stretch → planted squash. Repeated smaller versions of the same silhouette do not count as a traditional-animation performance.

## Exact-frame video audit — balanced timing pass

Each effect was rendered into exact normalized frames, encoded at 24 fps, and reviewed as both an MP4 and a complete contact sheet. The over-compressed pass exposed a shared problem: the graphic extreme often lasted only one or two frames, while disappearance or neutral registration occupied nearly half the clip. That distribution felt hurried rather than traditionally timed.

| Effect | Video finding | Balanced revision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Smear | Take, long pose, brake, and restoration were compressed into roughly six frames, followed by a long neutral tail. | 700 ms. Reach the long drawing around 16%, carry only through 28%, and begin the shortened brake immediately instead of adding a coast gap. |
| Suck In | The broad block became a wedge almost immediately and vanished halfway through the clip, leaving too many empty frames. | 900 ms. Drive body travel and edge elongation from the same pull curve so the complete mass is already moving on the first stretched drawing; preserve block and wedge exposures and close in the final fifth. |
| Spit Out | The spear appeared briefly, but the normal block arrived too early and occupied most of the sequence. | 900 ms. Allocate separate spear, wedge, and flight windows, then register the original block directly without a landing bounce. |
| Launch Away | Only about five captured frames contained the object before it cleared the stage. | 700 ms. Derive axial stretch, height compression, taper, and rear follow from traveled distance using an independently tunable stretch distance. The front receives the added stretch distance, while the rear advances right and increases its follow ratio through the same progression. Stretch may not finish before clearance begins. |
| Stretch Wipe | The initial full-height seed strip existed for only one frame; the surface was almost complete by the third. | 850 ms. Begin braking and monotonic size restoration together at 43%, before the leading edge reaches its stopping extreme. Center travel, width, height, taper, front arc, and rear catch must all lose energy across the same 43–74% interval. No contact hold, global width undershoot, or separate restoration pose is allowed. |
| Stamp In | Hang, fall, contact, and squash were all visible, but each received too little separation to establish weight. | 800 ms. Preserve the hang, but overlap the last falling drawings with the first compression drawings so impact never becomes a dead handoff. |

Traditional timing here means **unequal but readable exposure**, not uniformly slow motion. Takes remain fast; only the drawings that explain the force receive additional time.

## Smear video review — 60 fps

The Smear study was captured as a 4096×2304, 60 fps screen recording and reviewed at normal speed, 4× slow motion, and as a 12 fps contact sheet. The recording reveals problems that were not clear from isolated pose screenshots:

- The action reads as a sequence of width changes more than a body accelerating through space. The rear boundary stays too close to the starting area while the front boundary expands, making the burst resemble a wipe.
- The transition into the smear extreme is too geometric. The surface becomes a clean tapered quadrilateral instead of a deliberately authored traditional-animation drawing.
- The taper changes character during braking, creating a flag-like or perspective-flip silhouette.
- The brake wave is visually ambiguous. Several columns recover together, so rear-first compression is not clearly readable at normal speed.
- The texture becomes so horizontally compressed that the label flickers between legible and illegible rather than contributing a stable graphic mass.
- The 36 px final reverse correction reads as a second translation after the stop.
- Replay resets from the destination DOM pose to the starting DOM pose before capture, producing a visible teleport when the study loops.

### Next Smear target

- Move the entire center of mass forward during burst; allow the front to lead, but do not leave the rear boundary planted.
- Replace the continuously changing trapezoid with one authored extreme silhouette held for approximately 2 frames at 24 fps.
- Keep the center texture band comparatively stable and place most distortion in the leading and trailing thirds.
- Make the braking contact unmistakable: plant the front, then propagate a narrow compression ridge from rear to front.
- Reduce final correction to 10–14 px and resolve it within 80–100 ms.
- Treat replay reset as a separate invisible setup step, or animate back only when a reversible demo is explicitly desired.

### Revised Smear video check

The earlier restrained 760 ms version was rendered as 47 exact animation frames, encoded at 60 fps, and reviewed at normal speed and 4× slow motion. The balanced 700 ms pass keeps the fast take but restores readable long, brake, and registration drawings without returning to the earlier slow ease-out.

- The rear boundary advances with the take, so the action reads as moving mass instead of a stationary wipe.
- The extreme silhouette uses three connected bands with a restrained 278 px total width and stays stable across its intended read.
- The center texture band retains approximately its original physical width; most longitudinal distortion is isolated to the leading and trailing 28% regions.
- Per-column brake delay increases from rear to front, producing a shallow traveling compression ridge with only a 12% height contribution.
- The leading edge no longer plants abruptly. It continues from 226 px through a slow 236 px drift before resolving to 218 px on one continuous Hermite trajectory.
- The remaining intentional compromise is label readability at peak speed: the texture stays connected, but typography is treated as graphic mass during the extreme.

## Shared rendering contract

### CT-01 — One visual identity

The first canvas frame and the last canvas frame must match the live DOM in position, size, typography, border, radius, color, and content. No substitute drawing, simplified silhouette, or separately drawn border is allowed.

### CT-02 — A continuous surface

The snapshot is uploaded as one texture and rendered through a connected 2D mesh. Adjacent cells share vertices and texture coordinates. The implementation must not assemble independent image strips; the border and glyphs must therefore bend with the surface rather than split at slice boundaries.

### CT-03 — Authored drawings without stepped travel

The trajectory is evaluated continuously at display refresh rate. Major position channels use shared-tangent cubic Hermite curves so velocity remains continuous across phase boundaries. Shape channels use explicit drawing tracks with authored values for take, extreme, opposing pose, and registration. Animation character comes from contrast between those drawings—not from restarting an easing curve, sampling a generic elastic pulse, or quantizing the entire motion clock.

### CT-03A — No easing islands

Adjacent labels such as anticipation, release, catch-up, and settle describe acting beats; they must not be implemented as independent clips that each ease to zero velocity. A single motion curve carries translation through those beats, while deformation envelopes overlap before and after each nominal boundary.

### CT-03B — Separate travel from accent cadence

Traditional character does not require quantizing the entire object trajectory. Primary translation, axial stretch, squash, and boundary travel remain continuous. Only restrained silhouette accents such as a directional tilt, compression ridge, corner flick, or tail snap use a held cadence:

- Smear and Launch Away: 24 fps drawing cadence.
- Suck In, Spit Out, and Stamp In: 18 fps drawing cadence.
- Stretch Wipe: 20 fps boundary cadence.

This separation prevents 60 fps gelatinous detail without turning the action into browser-style frame skipping. Cadence is necessary but not sufficient: adjacent accents must differ in silhouette, spacing, or line of action for an intentional reason. A sampled sine wave or generic squash pulse still fails this rule.

### CT-03C — Exposures need connective in-betweens

Hard sampling a smooth procedural curve creates dropped-frame motion, not traditional animation. Each optional accent exposure is divided into two unequal parts:

- The first 44–50% preserves the key drawing long enough to read its graphic intent.
- The final 50–56% is a short eased in-between that carries the same directional force into the next drawing.
- Center-of-mass travel and the dominant deformation envelope never adopt the exposure clock; both continue on velocity-controlled trajectories.
- Secondary parts may arrive one drawing later than the leading action, but they may not reverse direction without an authored recoil or contact reason.

The result should read as **held intention connected by decisive motion**, not as either 60 fps rubber interpolation or a slideshow of disconnected poses.

### CT-03D — Forces overlap instead of handing off

Named phases describe what the audience reads, not mutually exclusive clips. Their channels overlap:

- Stretch begins during acceleration, not after translation has completed.
- Braking compression begins while the leading edge is still coasting.
- Squash starts at contact while downward momentum is still resolving.
- Recoil begins before compression has fully disappeared.
- A trailing edge catches up while the leading edge is already registering.

All dominant scalar tracks use monotone C1-continuous tangents. Velocity may reach zero at a genuine extreme or planted contact, but it may not reset merely because a timeline label changes.

### CT-03E — Speed is unequal spacing

Traditional speed does not come from shortening every phase equally. The velocity profile must spend time where the drawing needs to read and remove time where force should feel instantaneous:

- Smear reaches most of its forward distance and axial stretch inside the first 110 ms of active motion.
- A speed silhouette may coast for two or three drawings without continuing to inflate.
- Braking is a short 75–105 ms opposing force, not a long symmetric ease-out.
- Recoil begins inside the braking window, then receives the remaining registration time.
- Complex material transfers remain slower than direct takes, but their inactive holds are shortened.

### CT-03F — Stretch rides the first displacement

Stretch is not a preparation clip that completes before travel begins. The first visible displacement and the first visible deformation must occur in the same action window:

- Direct takes begin translating on the first active frame and reach their speed silhouette within roughly 12–28% of total time.
- Pull and release effects start edge capture while their tongue or spear is still extending.
- Wipe boundaries separate immediately; the leading edge does not finish stretching before the trailing edge starts moving.
- Impact effects acquire their contact stretch during the final approach, then compress only after the planted edge is established.

Acceptance: removing the texture deformation should still reveal a valid motion trajectory, and removing translation should still reveal a short directional shape change. When combined, neither channel may read as waiting for the other.

### CT-03G — Extreme poses release early

The extreme deformation is an accent, not the majority of the animation. Direct effects now follow this timing envelope:

- Reach the principal stretch within approximately 60–125 ms.
- Carry it only long enough for two or three readable drawings.
- Begin restoration while translation or braking is still active.
- Recover the original silhouette by roughly 55–84% of total time, leaving the final interval for positional registration rather than visible shape wobble.

Material-transfer effects may retain a wedge or spear longer because the object has not yet fully re-formed, but their source-to-destination travel must still accelerate immediately.

### CT-03H — Shared fast-take rhythm

All six studies now use the same performance grammar as Smear, adapted to their physical event:

1. **Immediate take:** displacement and directional deformation start together in the first 2–5%.
2. **Fast extreme:** the graphic speed, wedge, spear, wipe span, or contact drawing arrives by roughly 9–30%.
3. **Brief carry:** the extreme remains stable only long enough to read.
4. **Short catch:** collapse, landing, trailing-edge catch-up, or squash begins before the action feels parked.
5. **Quiet registration:** visible deformation ends early; the remaining time only completes placement or disappearance.

The effects may have different silhouettes and destinations, but none may return to a slow multi-stage procedural rhythm after the initial take.

Only an effect currently occupying the viewing area may auto-play. Multiple offscreen canvas animations must never compete for the same frames. Manual replay remains available for every study.

### CT-04 — Directional deformation

Deformation is not uniform scaling. Each vertex responds according to its distance from the leading edge, trailing edge, source, or destination. The object may form a controlled C-curve or S-curve, but must preserve a readable flow from one edge to the other.

### CT-05 — Handoff safety

The live element remains available to accessibility APIs. It becomes visually hidden only after the canvas texture is ready. At completion, the canvas reaches a DOM-compatible pose before the live element is revealed.

### CT-06 — No elastic or liquid default

The connected mesh is a rendering method, not a material simulation. Unless an effect explicitly calls for rubber or fluid, deformation must obey these limits:

- A channel may reverse once for a physically motivated brake or registration, but may not oscillate around its target.
- Squash returns directly to neutral; it does not pass through a taller rebound and a second undershoot.
- Directional slants keep one sign through the action instead of fluttering left and right.
- Per-column delay describes edge priority, not a traveling sine wave.
- The center band remains comparatively rigid while leading and trailing regions carry most deformation.

Acceptance: viewed without the labels, the object should read as a firm illustrated card acted upon by force—not jelly, cloth, water, or a spring.

## Smear

**Intent:** a classic hand-drawn animation take whose speed is expressed by strong axial stretch followed by a rear-to-front braking wave. The pose is not a replacement drawing: it is the original HTML texture deformed on one connected mesh.

| Phase | Time | Motion specification |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Read | 0–5% | Use only a registration beat. There is no visible reverse-loading action. |
| Mass take | 5–18% | Cover most forward spacing and reach the 168% speed drawing in the first active drawings. |
| Smear pose | 18–38% | Carry one low, long silhouette without continuing to inflate it. |
| Brake | 38–55% | Compress rear-to-front in one short opposing action while the nose is still coasting. |
| Recoil / register | 55–100% | Begin the tall recoil before braking fully resolves, then quietly match the HTML pose. |

Acceptance: the sequence must read as **normal → long/low → compact/low → tall recoil → normal**, not as one elastic width pulse. The leading edge coasts while rear texture catches up; the compression ridge is narrow and temporary; the full original texture, border, and typography remain connected.

## Launch Away

**Intent:** a direct, rapidly accelerating exit with the economy and exaggeration of an old animation take, using the same HTML texture through offscreen clearance.

| Phase | Time | Motion specification |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Release | 0–28% | Depart immediately through a nearly rigid drawing and a compact 148% take. No reverse translation or preparation hold. |
| Speed drawing | 28–74% | Hold one 186% wide, 56% high aerodynamic drawing. Keep the nose solid and make the trailing edge thinner. |
| Clear | 74–100% | The connected trailing edge exits the stage; do not dissolve or replace the artwork. |

Acceptance: forward movement begins on the first active frame; shape change overlaps the first translation; acceleration increases through the launch; no reverse load, drawing swap, or opacity fade.

## Stretch Wipe

**Intent:** reveal the original HTML surface as if a cel were pulled between two moving vertical boundaries and laid across the destination. Texture coordinates remain linear across the surface at every frame.

| Phase | Time | Motion specification |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Seed | 0–14% | Begin as one narrow, full-height rectangular strip. Do not collapse the texture to a point. |
| Write | 14–56% | The leading boundary accelerates across through alternating straight edge slants: positive, negative, positive, then negative. |
| Catch-up | 42–86% | Hold the front 12 px beyond the destination while the trailing boundary closes the stretched span. |
| Register | 86–100% | One 3–4 px opposing correction resolves both boundaries to the exact live HTML edges. |

Acceptance: this is a connected-surface reveal, not a clip-path over an already stationary element; every mesh column uses linear interpolation between the two boundaries, preventing broken borders and locally stretched glyph fragments; boundary motion overlaps; final canvas frame must match the DOM.

## Stamp In

**Intent:** an entrance that lands like a stamped animation drawing: rigid approach, contact, delayed compression, rebound, and a tiny registration correction.

| Phase | Time | Motion specification |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Hang | 0–16% | Hold an oversized pose above the stage to establish weight. |
| Drop / contact | 16–52% | Accelerate down; contact uses a deliberately narrow 96% wide, 108% tall drawing. |
| Stamp | 52–72% | Plant the bottom edge, then switch to a broad 132% wide, 64% high squash drawing. |
| Rebound | 72–82% | Change line of action with a 92% wide, 116% tall recoil and a 2 px opposing registration shift. |
| Settle | 82–100% | Pass through a smaller wide undershoot, then resolve exactly to the live HTML pose. |

Acceptance: contact and maximum squash must not share a frame; the bottom edge stays planted during compression; the original texture and outline remain connected; registration jitter decays rather than looping.

## Suck In

**Intent:** the destination captures the near edge and pulls the same material through a narrowing funnel against visible resistance.

| Phase | Time | Motion specification |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Resist | 0–16% | Object leans 7 px away while the target-facing edge reaches as a narrow straight tongue. |
| Edge capture | 16–34% | The tongue connects before the broad mass advances. Avoid bowing the whole body. |
| Funnel | 34–66% | Use distinct block and wedge drawings; captured columns advance in broad groups rather than a continuous liquid gradient. |
| Tail whip | 66–94% | Remaining far edge changes into one thin spear drawing and snaps to the point. |
| Point closure | 88–100% | The final connected pixels converge at the target, hold for one drawing, then disappear. |

Acceptance: destination-facing pixels always arrive first; the original texture stays legible until the funnel phase; no independent slices, border gaps, or `scale(0)` shortcut; the tail must show a final acceleration.

## Spit Out

**Intent:** compressed material erupts from a source, unfolds into its original HTML silhouette, and absorbs excess energy on landing. It is not Suck In in reverse.

| Phase | Time | Motion specification |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Pressure | 0–10% | A 1–3 px textured point pulses at the source without revealing a substitute drawing. |
| Spear | 10–30% | Leading texture fires as a thin straight spear; the source end remains pinched. |
| Wedge / unfurl | 30–58% | A broad wedge restores height and content front-to-back without a sinusoidal body wave. |
| Silhouette catch | 58–76% | Full content becomes readable with a 12 px forward overshoot before arrival. |
| Landing | 76–90% | Contact drawing precedes a 72% high squash by at least one exposure. Bottom edge remains planted. |
| Settle | 90–100% | A 110% tall rebound and 96% undershoot resolve to the exact DOM pose. |

Acceptance: the source end must lag; filament velocity must be highest immediately after release; normal silhouette must return before landing squash; landing and squash may not peak on the same frame.

## Prototype validation checklist

- Replay each effect at 1× and 0.5×.
- Confirm canvas handoff produces no flash.
- Inspect border corners and glyph strokes during maximum deformation.
- Confirm first/last frames align with the DOM reference.
- Resize the viewport and replay; capture dimensions must be recalculated per run.
- Verify reduced-motion mode shows a short cross-position transition without extreme deformation.
